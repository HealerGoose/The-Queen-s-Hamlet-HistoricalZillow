Property Overview

Welcome to the Hameau de la Reine (Queen's Hamlet), a historic retreat commissioned by Marie Antoinette in 1783 and completed in 1786. Located near the Petit Trianon on the grounds of Versailles, the Hamlet was designed by architect Richard Mique.

Built around an artificial lake, the village included cottages, a dairy, a farm, a decorative mill, and the Queen's House. Although designed to appear rustic, the interiors were luxurious and reflected the tastes of the French aristocracy.

The Hamlet embodied the Enlightenment-era fascination with nature and the late eighteenth-century "return to nature" movement. Today it remains one of the most famous sites associated with Marie Antoinette and the final years of the French monarchy.

Historical Significance

Although intended as a peaceful retreat, the Hamlet later became a symbol of royal privilege. Many critics viewed it as evidence that the monarchy was disconnected from the struggles of ordinary French citizens during a period of economic hardship.

Historians study the Queen's Hamlet because it helps explain aristocratic culture, Enlightenment ideas, public opinion, and the growing tensions that contributed to the French Revolution.
